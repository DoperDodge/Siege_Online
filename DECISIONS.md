| D-029 | 2026-10-01 | **Netcode model (PLAN §5):** the server simulates at 64 Hz and sends snapshots every 2nd tick (32 Hz). The client predicts its own bodies with the shared simulation and sends, with each input, a hash of the state it predicted; the server corrects (exact float32 state, a new epoch) only when its own result differs. Other players are sent quantized (positions to 2 mm, view angles to 0.01°) as deltas against the last snapshot *sent* (TCP delivers in order), with a baseline checksum every 64 snapshots and a full resend on mismatch. | About 15 kbps down per player with 10 in a room (netsim), and no correction traffic unless something actually mispredicts. | Active |
| D-030 | 2026-10-01 | **Input timing:** each snapshot carries how many of your inputs the server has queued; the client stretches or shrinks its tick period by up to 5 % to keep about 2 queued. Every server tick earns one input credit (max 16) and each applied input spends one, so a backlog after a stall is applied at most one extra input per tick and a client can't move faster than real time. **A player whose next input hasn't arrived is held still** (not stepped at all) instead of moved with a guessed input. The hold is a budget of 16 ticks (250 ms): each held tick spends one, each applied input earns back a quarter; once it is spent the body carries on without input (gravity, a vault in progress), so nobody can hang in mid-air by withholding all or most of their inputs. Those steps spend input credit too, so catching up never puts a body ahead of room time. A new body waiting for its client (joining, a respawn in flight) is never stepped without input. The queue holds up to 32 inputs. After a new body (join, respawn, operator pick), inputs sent for the old body are dropped. | Any guessed input is a misprediction the client must be corrected for; holding still made short stalls cost no corrections (netsim: 3–7 corrections per 30 s down to ~1). | Active |
| D-031 | 2026-10-01 | **Lag compensation:** shots ride on inputs (the fire button, `Btn.Fire`): the server judges a shot after applying the input that fired it, along that input's view, and rewinds everyone else to the time that input says the shooter was drawing (its newest snapshot tick minus its interpolation delay). It uses the same quantized snapshot states and interpolation the client drew. The claimed delay is bounded by the client's own interpolation ceiling (150 ms), the claimed snapshot by the least lag the connection has shown in the last two seconds plus 4 ticks, and the whole rewind is capped at 200 ms (250 ms since D-045) counted from when the input arrived (the time it then waits in our queue is ours, not the shooter's latency) (PLAN §5). As in other shooters, a client that consistently fakes lag can get up to the cap and no more. ~~Shots are spaced by the shooter's own ticks (at least 8 apart)~~ (superseded by D-040: the simulation fires at the weapon's own rate), so a backlog applied after a stall keeps every shot; Skopós can't fire while looking through a shell's camera. | The server judges exactly what the shooter saw and aimed at; beyond the cap, a very laggy player has to lead targets instead of everyone else getting shot behind cover. | Active |

| D-041 | 2026-10-07 | **Aiming, recoil and spread.** ADS progress is part of the predicted body state: it rises over the weapon's ADS time (class time ÷ (1 + sight and laser bonuses), ×1.1 straight out of a sprint) and falls over 0.25 s; it drops during a weapon swap and in a fall longer than 1 m (Siege X takes you out of ADS in long drops; the height is a placeholder). Accuracy follows the class's ADS curve.
